Ruptured MCA Aneurysm in a Neonate

Presentation and imaging

A 10-day-old boy born at 37 weeks presented with hypothermia, poor feeding and seizures. CT showed left convexity subdural blood, left insular intraparenchymal blood and frontal and temporal subarachnoid blood extending from the Sylvian fissure. There was roughly 7 mm of left-to-right midline shift. CTA showed a posteriorly directed 10 × 7 × 8 mm aneurysm on a small insular branch of the superior division of the left middle cerebral artery (MCA). The sagittal CTA views helped trace the inflow and a small outflow branch. [1]

Published neonatal cases commonly present with seizures. Transfontanelle ultrasound can help detect blood, and vascular imaging can define the lesion and its parent vessel. Hemorrhage can involve more than one compartment. The lesion and its relationship to adjacent hematoma matter to the treatment plan, particularly when clot causes mass effect. [2]

Sylvian anatomy and the target

The Sylvian fissure separates the frontal and temporal opercula over the insula. The proximal sphenoidal M1 segment gives rise to the MCA divisions, which course over the insular surface as M2 branches. Lenticulostriate branches around proximal MCA supply deep brain structures. The aneurysm involved a small insular branch rather than the MCA division itself. Its fusiform shape involved a segment of that branch, so the team identified the vessel on both sides of the lesion. [1] [3]

Why open treatment was selected

Open surgery let the team address the risk of rerupture and evacuate clot during the same operation. The video describes consideration of observation and endovascular parent-vessel occlusion, with substantial mass effect and durability influencing the choice. Vessel injury, ischemia, insular or basal ganglia injury, and blood loss in a 3.3 kg neonate were particular concerns. The team prepared blood and used somatosensory evoked potential (SSEP) monitoring. [1]

From temporary control to branch exclusion

The left-sided two-piece craniotomy used the coronal suture and kept an anterior osteoplastic flap attached. After durotomy and early subdural clot evacuation, the team pursued the proximal Sylvian fissure and applied temporary M1 control. Distal opening then exposed the thrombosed aneurysm. Temporary control was released before permanent clipping of the branch inflow. The team opened the sac to check for residual flow, divided the branch on both sides of the lesion and resected the isolated segment. The distal coagulation and division are described in the recording and omitted from the microscope footage. [1]

Reported outcome and evidence limits

The authors report stable SSEPs, no further seizures during reported follow-up, no appreciable neurological deficit and no residual aneurysm on the six-month CTA. The video describes planned surveillance. This case records one operation and its early outcome. Its result cannot estimate the comparative benefit of microsurgical and endovascular approaches or the child's long-term neurodevelopment. The 3D view uses the native adult Z-Anatomy skull, cortex and main MCA segments. The small diseased branch, hemorrhage and motion are teaching reconstructions of the neonatal operation. [1] [2]
